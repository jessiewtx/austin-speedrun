# BrainLift: GT School — Hostable Academic Events (Austin)

## Owners
- Jessie

## Purpose
Evaluate which academic events GT School can host in Austin as a **live-event funnel** for SpeedRun / Timeback, comparing cost-to-host, draw (turnout), and audience fit, so we can pick what to run first. Researched bottom-up: sourced facts (DOK 1/2) → insights (DOK 3) → recommendations (DOK 4).

## In Scope
- Established competition formats GT can host or partner on (math leagues/olympiads, spelling bee) + a DIY hackathon.
- **Host-side cost** (what GT pays), participation/draw data, and Austin venue costs.

## Out of Scope
- Family-facing pricing (what schools charge students), except to distinguish it from host cost.
- The SpeedRun contest mechanics themselves (covered elsewhere); non-academic events.

---

## DOK 4: Spiky Points of View (SPOVs)

- **SPOV 1 — Lead with AMC 8 or MathLeague; shelve the spelling bee.** Math out-draws spelling by ~3–6× at the event level and costs a fraction to host; a Scripps regional partnership is $5,500 for a structurally tiny field.
  - *Elaboration:* Spelling funnels to ~243 spellers nationally and tens at a regional bee (2.5), while AMC 8 alone puts ~80k kids into events yearly (2.1) and Austin/Texas is unusually math-dense (2.4). AMC 8/MathLeague host fees are ~$430–500 at 150 kids (1.1, 1.2) vs $5,500 flat for Scripps (1.5). (2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 1.1, 1.2, 1.5)
- **SPOV 2 — These events are the right *bridge* activity because they're unblocked.** Hosting an established contest "presented by GT School" needs no gt.school domain and no MAP/legal ruling.
  - *Elaboration:* Every format below is run by a third party that already owns the test, rules, and brand; GT only supplies venue + proctors + marketing (1.1–1.6), so it can run *now* while SpeedRun is stuck on domain/legal/Timeback dependencies.
- **SPOV 3 — Math Kangaroo is a reach play, not a middle-school play.** It's big, but ~60% of its U.S. field is grades 1–4.
  - *Elaboration:* Kangaroo's draw is real (~54–60k) but skews young (2.2); for a *middle-school* funnel, AMC 8 (grades ≤8) and MathLeague's MS division are better-targeted (1.2, 1.1). (2.2, 1.1, 1.2)

## DOK 3: Insights

- **Insight 1 — At the competitive-event level, math dwarfs spelling.** ~80k AMC 8 + ~65k MATHCOUNTS + ~54–60k Kangaroo + ~30k MathLeague participants/yr vs 243 Scripps national spellers (and all of Texas sending 22). (2.1, 2.2, 2.4, 2.5)
- **Insight 2 — AMC 8 and MathLeague are the cheapest to host; Kangaroo and Scripps are pricier per outcome.** AMC 8 ≈ $2.50/kid + $55–115 flat; MathLeague ≈ $500 site license (100+); Kangaroo is $20/kid; Scripps regional is $5,500 flat. (1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5)
- **Insight 3 — The competition fee is the small part; GT's real cost is its own venue/food/prizes.** The license is $430–3,000; venue+food+prizes for ~150 kids adds ~$1,750–4,000 (Palmer pushes it far higher). (1.1–1.5, 3.1, 3.2)
- **Insight 4 — Austin/Texas is a strong host market for math specifically.** Texas has 23 MATHCOUNTS chapters and had the largest Scripps national delegation (22), and Texas is a top Kangaroo state — competition culture is deep here. (2.4, 2.5)

### GT host-cost analysis (derived from the DOK 1 cost facts above)
"GT pays" = competition fee (fixed by the org) + GT's own event costs (its choice). At ~150 kids, cheap/free venue:

|---|---|---|---|---|

## DOK 2: Knowledge Tree

### Category 1: Hostable formats & host cost

#### Subcategory 1.1: MathLeague (host your own contest)
- **Source 1:** "mathleague.org" (official site, host form & on-demand pricing) + AdmissionSight guide
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Runs 400+ contests/yr reaching 30,000+ students, grades 3–12.
  - School membership fee eliminated beginning 2024–25.
  - Standard contest fee: $20/student on-time + one $5 processing fee per group ($30/student if late).
  - Tiered pricing down to $5/student for groups of 48+.
  - Schools with 100+ students may buy a $500 site license for unlimited students on one test set.
  - Hosting is via a "contest host form"; host becomes "contest director"; modes are in-person, live-online, or on-demand; host picks the date.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - MathLeague lets any organization host a local math contest on a date it chooses by submitting a host form and providing the venue/proctors while MathLeague supplies the problem sets. It's cheap and flexible: ~$5–20 per student or a flat $500 site license for 100+, with no membership fee since 2024–25.
- **Link to source:** [mathleague.org](https://www.mathleague.org/) · [host form](https://mathleague.org/hostform.php) · [pricing](https://mathleague.org/ondemand.php) · [AdmissionSight guide](https://admissionsight.com/math-league-contests/)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 1.2: AMC 8 (Mathematical Association of America)
- **Source 1:** MAA AMC registration page + 2026 AMC 8 Teacher's Manual + Texas CBE parent guide
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - For students in grade 8 and below (under ~14.5–15.5 on test day).
  - Hosted by "schools, universities, learning centers, math circles"; home schools are not allowed; legitimacy verified at registration; run by a Competition Manager (an adult unrelated to participants).
  - Per-competition registration fee (2026–27): $55 early (by Oct 28, 2026), $75 regular (by Jan 5, 2027), $115 late.
  - Student licenses: $2.50/participant, sold in bundles of 10 for $25 (digital or print-and-scan).
  - 2027 competition window: Jan 21–27, 2027.
  - MAA permits the host to charge students a small fee to cover administration; individuals cannot register directly with MAA.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - AMC 8 is the prestigious entry point to the AMC pipeline; a "learning center" like GT can host it as a vetted Competition Manager. MAA's own cost is low, $2.50/student plus a $55–115 flat registration fee, so effective host cost is ~$2.70–4.30/kid; the higher $10–30 figures people cite are what host sites charge families, not MAA's price.
- **Link to source:** [MAA AMC registration](https://maa.org/amcreg/) · [2026 AMC 8 Teacher's Manual](https://ivyleaguecenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-amc-8-teachers-manual.pdf) · [Texas CBE guide](https://www.texascbe.com/blog/amc-8-complete-guide-for-parents)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 1.3: Math Kangaroo (become a Center)
- **Source 1:** "How to Start a Math Kangaroo Center" (Math Kangaroo USA)
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Free to apply to become a Center; 10-student minimum.
  - Student regular registration fee $20 ($35 late); Title I schools 50% off.
  - Eligibility K–12; paper or digital.
  - "Open registration" (in-person) centers are listed publicly and may accept any student from any school.
  - 2026–27 cycle: new-center applications open ~Apr 1, 2026; school registration deadline ~Dec 15, 2026; competition ~Mar 18, 2027.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - Math Kangaroo is free to host (apply as a Center) but costs $20/student, notably higher per-kid than AMC 8 or MathLeague. Its "open registration" option publicly lists the host and lets any local kid sign up, which is useful for community reach.
- **Link to source:** [Math Kangaroo — Start a Center](https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/resources/how-to-start-math-kangaroo-center/)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 1.4: MOEMS (Math Olympiad, Elem/Middle)
- **Source 1:** MOEMS overview (Institute of Competition Sciences)
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Five monthly contests, November–March, for teams of up to 35 students, grades 4–8.
  - Only schools or homeschool associations (not individuals) may register; teams from more than one school aren't eligible for team awards.
  - Team registration runs ~$175–300 per team of 35 depending on registration date; registration opens ~May.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - MOEMS is a team-based, season-long olympiad (5 contests) rather than a one-day event, giving a recurring touchpoint with a committed cohort. Cost is modest per team (~$175–300 for up to 35 kids) but it commits GT to a multi-month series.
- **Link to source:** [MOEMS overview (competitionsciences.org)](https://www.competitionsciences.org/competitions/math-olympiads/)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 1.5: Scripps National Spelling Bee (host cost)
- **Source 1:** Scripps "Bee a Regional Partner" sheet + Educator Guide
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Regional Partnership is a one-year exclusive agreement; fee starts at $5,500 (covers materials, Bee staff support, online testing platform, $18 credit per enrolled school, and hotel + Bee Week for the champion plus a guardian).
  - Additional advancing spellers cost +$3,500 each.
  - School-level enrollment (to run a school bee) is $199/school ($185 early).
  - Regional competition season is February–March.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - To *own a region* Scripps requires a $5,500+ exclusive regional partnership; to merely run a school bee it's $199. The regional route is a real program to administer, high cost relative to the small competitive headcount it yields.
- **Link to source:** [Scripps Regional Partner sheet (2026)](https://spellingbee.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/Bee_RegionalPartnerSheet_2026.pdf) · [2026 Educator Guide](https://spellingbee.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/2026%20Educator%20Guide_B.pdf)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 1.6: DIY Hackathon (no franchise)
- **Source 1:** HackHQ budget guide + College Board Hackathon Event Guide
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Rule-of-thumb budget: $50–75/participant in-person ($15–30 virtual); add 10% contingency.
  - Component costs: food ~$40–50/person; external venue $500–2,000+; prizes $500–3,000; swag $12–15/person.
  - Recommended space: a large central room (gym/cafeteria/auditorium) with reliable WiFi, power, tables, projector; ≥3 staff.
  - HS hackathon budgets range from ~$500 to ~$75,000.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - A hackathon is fully GT-built (no franchise fee) but food/prize-heavy, so all-in it's the priciest per head at ~$50–75/participant. It offers maximum brand control and an "innovation" flavor, at the cost of more effort and no built-in participant pipeline.
- **Link to source:** [HackHQ budget guide](https://hackhq.io/planning-guide/budget) · [College Board Hackathon Toolkit](https://elective.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/hackathon-toolkit.pdf)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

### Category 2: Participation / draw

#### Subcategory 2.1: AMC 8 participation
- **Source 1:** AoPS Wiki "AMC 8" (uses MAA data) + MAA AMC statistics
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - ~80,000 U.S. students take the AMC 8 annually (student counts: 80,725 in 2024; 79,524 in 2025; 80,977 recent).
  - 2024 field spanned 2,053 schools.
  - Average score ~10–12 of 25; top-5% "Honor Roll" ≈ top ~4,000 students; top-1% "Distinction" ≈ top ~800.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - AMC 8 reliably draws ~80,000 U.S. middle-schoolers a year across ~2,000 schools (~40/school), making it a large, well-established math-competition audience. (Some third-party pages cite higher global figures; MAA's own student count is ~80k.)
- **Link to source:** [AoPS Wiki — AMC 8](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_8) · [MAA AMC statistics](https://amc-reg.maa.org/reports/GeneralReports.aspx)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 2.2: Math Kangaroo participation
- **Source 1:** Gonit "Math Kangaroo Participation" + Math Kangaroo USA + Think Academy
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - U.S. participation was 53,562 in 2025 (record) and 60,504 in 2026, across 1,682 U.S. centers (2026).
  - Grades 1–4 accounted for over 32,000 of the ~53,562 (2025), i.e., ~60%.
  - California led with ~21,000 (2025); Texas is among the next-largest states.
  - Globally ~6.5 million students in 90+ countries (largest math competition worldwide).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - Math Kangaroo USA is sizable and growing (~54–60k) but heavily elementary-skewed (~60% grades 1–4), so its middle-school slice is smaller than the headline number. Texas is a strong state for it.
- **Link to source:** [Gonit — Math Kangaroo participation](https://gonit.app/blog/math-kangaroo-participation/) · [Math Kangaroo USA](https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/) · [Think Academy 2025 results](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/math-kangaroo-2025-results-scores-awards-and-rankings/)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 2.3: MOEMS participation
- **Source 1:** MOEMS overview (Institute of Competition Sciences)
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - 80,462 students had recorded scores in the 2025–26 season.
  - Teams are up to 35 students; many schools run multiple teams; grades 4–8.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - MOEMS engages ~80,000 students a year through school/homeschool teams, a large elementary-and-middle audience, but delivered as a season of five contests rather than a single big event.
- **Link to source:** [MOEMS (competitionsciences.org)](https://www.competitionsciences.org/competitions/math-olympiads/)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 2.4: MATHCOUNTS participation (national + Texas)
- **Source 1:** MATHCOUNTS 2024–25 Annual Report + 2026 National Fact Sheet + TSPE (Texas)
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - 64,817 students participated at 3,724 schools in the Competition Series (2024–25); grades 6–8.
  - Texas has 23 local MATHCOUNTS chapters (organized by TSPE); chapter competitions held throughout February.
  - The 2026 Texas State Competition hosted ~279 mathletes (advancing from chapters).
  - Nationally, the top 4 students per state advance to the National Competition (224 competitors).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - MATHCOUNTS is the flagship U.S. middle-school (6–8) math competition, ~65,000 students across ~3,700 schools, and Texas is well-organized with 23 chapters, evidence of deep local competition-math infrastructure to tap.
- **Link to source:** [MATHCOUNTS 2024–25 Annual Report](https://www.mathcounts.org/about/2024-2025-mathcounts-annual-report) · [2026 National Fact Sheet](https://www.mathcounts.org/sites/default/files/2026%20National%20Competition%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf) · [TSPE MATHCOUNTS (Texas)](https://www.tspe.org/page/MATHCOUNTS)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 2.5: Scripps Spelling Bee participation / draw
- **Source 1:** Scripps 2025 press release + 2025 Media Guide + Educator Guide
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - The 2025 national finals had 243 spellers total.
  - National field skews middle-school: 67 seventh-graders and 105 eighth-graders (of 243).
  - Texas had the largest state delegation, 22 national competitors; California 20; Ohio 15.
  - National qualifiers advance through 200+ regional partners and 225+ regional champions.
  - The overall program reaches "millions of students" across 20,000+ schools at the classroom/school level.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - Spelling is enormous at the classroom level but funnels to a very small competitive field, 243 at nationals, and only ~one champion per school at each regional bee. So an actual GT-hosted spelling *event* would draw far fewer kids than a math contest, even though the program touches millions upstream.
- **Link to source:** [Scripps 2025 press release (243 spellers)](https://scripps.com/press-releases/scripps-national-spelling-bee-welcomes-243-spellers-for-historic-100th-anniversary/) · [2025 Media Guide](https://scripps.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Bee_2025MediaGuide-1.pdf)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

### Category 3: Austin venues & cost

#### Subcategory 3.1: Palmer Events Center
- **Source 1:** Palmer Events Center (Plan / Facts / Facility Rental)
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Downtown Austin (900 Barton Springs Rd); 70,000 sq ft of column-free exhibit hall + five 1,000 sq ft meeting rooms; adjacent 1,200-space garage ($8/vehicle); free WiFi.
  - Meeting rooms rent ~$125/day each.
  - Exhibit-hall / conference daily rental ranges ~$2,150–$10,500 depending on space and configuration (conference use ~$2,600–$7,200/day).
  - Add technical labor ~$150/hr and electrical labor ~$65/hr.
  - Quotes via 512-404-4500 (priced by event type).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - Palmer is the strong "flagship/championship" option downtown, big, well-equipped, skyline views, but at $2,600–7,200/day for hall space plus labor it's an order of magnitude pricier than a small meeting room ($125/day) or a borrowed venue.
- **Link to source:** [Palmer Events Center — Plan](https://www.palmereventscenter.com/plan) · [Facts](https://www.palmereventscenter.com/palmer-events-center-facts) · [Facility Rental rates](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/39302181/facility-rental-palmer-events-center)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)

#### Subcategory 3.2: Austin Convention Center (city rate schedule)
- **Source 1:** City of Austin Convention Center rate ordinance
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Exhibit-hall daily rentals run roughly $2,300–$8,300/day depending on hall.
  - Meeting rooms are ~$125/day each.
  - Move-in/move-out days charged at one-half the daily rate.
  - Directly network-connected to Palmer Events Center.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:**
  - The city-run Austin Convention Center is the larger downtown option with a published rate schedule in the same ballpark as Palmer (halls in the low-to-high thousands per day, meeting rooms ~$125/day), best reserved for a large flagship rather than a pilot.
- **Link to source:** [City of Austin Convention Center rate ordinance](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=99116)  (accessed Aug 1, 2026)
