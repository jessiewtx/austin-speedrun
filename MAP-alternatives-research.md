# MAP Replacement Options for the Austin Speedrun (Timeback efficacy measure)

## Purpose
NWEA MAP is our current external yardstick, but we (likely) can't use MAP scores in a public, cash-prize contest because of NWEA's licensing/MSA terms (commercial-use restriction). This doc compares the real alternatives and recommends what to use.

**The bar isn't "can it measure growth."** The whole reason we need an outside test is **credibility**: an independent, nationally-normed, proctored score is what makes "Timeback works" believable to a skeptic. A test Timeback controls (our own XP/mastery data) proves nothing to an outsider, and a test nobody in our market recognizes is nearly as weak. Every option is judged on four things:

1. **Independent & verifiable** (third-party, nationally normed, proctored)
2. **Recognized & trusted in our market** (Austin metro / Texas) — *not* just "TEA-approved on paper"
3. **Measures growth** across a season (pre/post), grades 6–8
4. **Licensable for a cash-prize contest** (the exact thing that blocks MAP)

---

## TL;DR recommendation

**Two-test stack:**
- **Headline proof + prize gate → SAT / PSAT 8/9 (College Board).** The only name on this list every Texas parent, kid, and reporter actually recognizes and trusts as *proof*. It's the proven talent-search model (Duke TIP, Johns Hopkins CTY, Northwestern CTD), College Board is built to license testing to outside programs, and it doubles as our SAT-unlock.
- **Growth engine (the MAP replacement) → Renaissance Star.** In our market it's the recognizable, neutral growth test (used locally by Pflugerville ISD, ubiquitous across TX via Accelerated Reader), with a real national growth metric (SGP). Preferred over i-Ready because it's more locally known **and** it isn't a competing curriculum.

**Fallback:** i-Ready only if testing kids **outside a single school** (homeschool/citywide) becomes the deciding factor — it's the only one runnable via an authorized-provider model. But it's effectively **unused in the Austin metro** and is a Timeback competitor, so it's the weakest on recognition and neutrality.

**Hard gate for all of them:** every non-College-Board option carries the *same* "can we use scores in a cash-prize contest?" licensing question that blocked MAP. Confirm commercial use in writing **before** committing. The College Board path is likely least fraught.

> **Local irony worth flagging:** the most-recognized growth test *in the Austin metro* is MAP itself — the one we can't use. If Legal ever clears MAP for commercial use, it's the strongest local option because families here already know it.

**How "Recognition" is rated:** there are two different questions. (1) *How many kids take it* — i-Ready (~14M) and MAP (~13M) are huge, Star is in 37k+ schools. (2) *Does our audience trust the score as proof* — only the **SAT** clears that bar publicly; the interim tests are ubiquitous but treated as routine classroom screeners. And **in Texas these interim tests are district-by-district (no state mandate)**, so recognition is hyper-local: in the **Austin metro specifically, MAP is the one families know, Star has a real footprint, and i-Ready is effectively absent.**

## Pros & cons (the obvious version)

- **SAT / PSAT 8/9 — best public proof + gate.** ➕ Most recognized/trusted by far; proven talent-search model; program-friendly licensing; already our SAT-unlock. ➖ Achievement not fine-grained growth; floor effects for younger/below-grade; PSAT 8/9 fits grade 8 best (above-level for 6–7).
- **Renaissance Star — best MAP-replacement growth engine.** ➕ Real SGP growth; recognizable in our market (Pflugerville + AR legacy); neutral (not a curriculum). ➖ School-based administration (friction for homeschoolers); same commercial-license question as MAP; low public trust as a credential.
- **i-Ready — fallback.** ➕ Strong growth model; the only one runnable outside a single school. ➖ Essentially unused in Austin; elementary-weighted; a Timeback competitor (bad optics as a judge); same license question.
- **NWEA MAP — blocked.** ➕ Everything (local recognition, growth, it's what Timeback already uses). ➖ The one thing that matters: (likely) can't license it for a cash-prize contest.
- **TerraNova / Iowa — honorable mention.** ➕ Some familiarity with private/homeschool families. ➖ Point-in-time achievement (weak for growth); unknown to the public.

---

## What Legal / Ops must confirm next
- **Commercial-use license** for a public cash-prize contest, per vendor (College Board, Renaissance, Curriculum Associates). This is the MAP-killer — get it in writing first.
- **Who administers & proctors** — can GT School order/administer as a school or test center? Cash prizes require proctoring/identity verification.
- **Two proctored windows** (start + end of season) for any growth claim.
- **Grade + floor coverage** for 6–8 and below-grade kids (favors Star/i-Ready) vs. elite-achievement proof (favors SAT/PSAT).

## DOK 2 Knowledge Tree — one category per test

### Test 1 — SAT / PSAT 8/9 (College Board)
- **Sources:** College Board 2025 SAT Suite reports (national + Texas) & newsroom; TEA SAT pages; College Board Texas partnership & PSAT 8/9 overview; Northwestern NUMATS (talent-search model); TEA approved-tests list.
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - **Over 2 million** students in the class of 2025 took the SAT at least once nationally (first time above 2M since 2020; 97% digital).
  - **Texas class of 2025: 301,020 SAT takers, a 74% participation rate** (of 408,251 grads). ~1.36M took the SAT via School Day nationally.
  - PSAT-related takers 2024–25: ~3.1M PSAT/NMSQT, ~358k PSAT 10, **~1.18M PSAT 8/9**.
  - PSAT 8/9 is designed for **grades 8–9** as a baseline to track progress to the SAT; talent searches (Duke TIP historically, now Northwestern's NUMATS/CTD) give **PSAT 8/9 above-level to grades 3–6** and **SAT/ACT to grades 7–9**, and note the tests "can be used to monitor academic growth."
  - **400+ Texas districts** have run SAT School Day; TEA has a statewide SAT/ACT contract + HB3 reimbursement. On TEA's approved group-administered achievement tests list.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:** The SAT suite is the only option here with genuine public recognition and trust — taken by ~74% of Texas grads and universally understood as a "real" test. It's the proven, ~50-year talent-search credibility model and the most program-friendly to license. Weaknesses: it's an achievement (point-in-time) test with floor effects for younger/below-grade kids, so it proves *elite achievement* better than *universal growth*, and PSAT 8/9 fits 8th grade cleanly (above-level for 6–7).
- **Links:** [College Board newsroom — 2M+ SAT takers](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/sat-participation-class-2025-surpasses-2-million-test-takers-first-time-2020) · [2025 Texas SAT Suite report (PDF)](https://reports.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/2025-texas-sat-suite-of-assessments-annual-report%20ADA-v0.2.pdf) · [TEA — SAT](https://tea.texas.gov/student-readiness-and-high-school/college-career-and-military-prep/sat) · [College Board — Texas partnership](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/state-partnerships/texas) · [PSAT 8/9 overview](https://counselors.collegeboard.org/assessments/psat-8-9/overview-dates) · [Northwestern NUMATS guide (PDF)](https://www.ctd.northwestern.edu/sites/default/files/2020-12/NUMATS%20Guide%20to%20Participation%20for%20Schools.pdf) (accessed Aug 3, 2026)

### Test 2 — NWEA MAP Growth *(reference — blocked for commercial use)*
- **Sources:** NWEA MAP Growth page + fact sheet; TEA approved-tests list + NWEA Texas–STAAR linking study; Austin-metro district pages; Alpha School.
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Serves **more than 13 million students**; **35,900+ schools, 4,500+ districts, 146 countries**.
  - Adaptive, K–12, given 2–3×/year in Math/Reading/Language/Science; growth via the **RIT scale + Conditional Growth Percentile/Index**.
  - TEA-approved ("Texas MAP Growth"); NWEA's **Texas–STAAR linking study covered 47,303 students across 278 schools in 75 Texas districts** (correlations > 0.70).
  - **Dominant interim screener in the Austin metro:** Austin ISD, Leander ISD (grades 6–8 take MAP Math + Reading), Hays CISD, and Georgetown ISD all use MAP Growth.
  - **Timeback/Alpha uses MAP as its external benchmark** (reporting students in the top 1–2% nationally, ~2× typical growth).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:** MAP is the strongest *local* option — the Austin-metro default that families already receive reports on — and the efficacy standard Timeback itself cites. It's blocked only by the commercial-use licensing question, which is the entire reason for this doc. Keep it in play as the fallback if Legal clears it.
- **Links:** [NWEA — MAP Growth](https://www.nwea.org/map-growth/) · [NWEA — Texas STAAR linking study (PDF)](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/TX-MAP-Growth-Linking-Study-Report_EOG_2025.pdf) · [Austin ISD — MAP Growth](https://www.austinisd.org/announcements/2021/10/06/new-tests-measure-student-growth-through-adaptive-technology) · [Leander ISD — Universal Screener](https://www.leanderisd.org/universalscreener/) · [Hays CISD — Universal Screeners](https://www.hayscisd.net/o/hcisd/page/universal-screeners-map-mclass) · [Georgetown ISD — Universal Screener](https://www.georgetownisd.org/parents/universal-screener) · [Alpha School — TimeBack](https://alpha.school/blog/introducing-timeback-the-next-evolution-of-alphas-model/) (accessed Aug 3, 2026)

### Test 3 — Renaissance Star
- **Sources:** Renaissance state-approvals + SGP documentation; HandWiki/Renaissance company figures; Pflugerville ISD improvement plan; TEA approved-tests list; comparison overview.
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Star is used in **37,000+ schools** (company calls it the most widely used computer-adaptive assessment in US pre-K–12); **~7 million Star assessments administered in 2021–22**; used in all 50 states, "especially common in Texas, Florida, Georgia," often paired with **Accelerated Reader** (~5.4M students / ~22,000 schools).
  - Growth via **Student Growth Percentile (SGP)**: norm-referenced, 1–99 (50 = typical), computed from ≥2 windows via quantile regression.
  - K–12, adaptive, short (15–30 min), Reading + Math; TEA-approved (Grade 7 reading list + group-administered list). **School-based only** (no direct external/homeschool option).
  - **Austin-metro presence: Pflugerville ISD uses Star360** as its universal screener (grades 1–9, math + reading).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:** The most recognizable *growth* alternative to MAP in our market — real local footprint (Pflugerville) plus deep TX familiarity through Accelerated Reader — and a clean, neutral yardstick (pure assessment, not a competing curriculum). Cons: school-based administration (a hurdle for homeschoolers / a cross-district contest) and the same commercial-use license question as MAP. As a *public credential* it's still low-trust (families experience it as routine screening).
- **Links:** [Renaissance — State approvals](https://www.renaissance.com/resources/state-approvals/) · [Renaissance — Student Growth Percentile](https://support.renaissance.com/s/article/Student-Growth-Percentile-SGP-1752674163265) · [Renaissance company figures (HandWiki)](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Company:Renaissance_Learning) · [Pflugerville ISD improvement plan (PDF)](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1732296840/pfisdnet/fd584hz138njvaz5vltv/DistrictImprovementPlan_SY23-24.pdf) (accessed Aug 3, 2026)

### Test 4 — i-Ready Diagnostic (Curriculum Associates)
- **Sources:** Curriculum Associates press releases; EdSource (independent); i-Ready growth-model + Texas STAAR correlation brief; Texas TIA vendor FAQ; Texas district case studies; comparison overview.
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - Curriculum Associates claims **~14 million students** (~⅓ of all K–8); independent reporting (EdSource) corroborates **>13 million** nationally.
  - Growth via **Typical Growth / Stretch Growth** vs national norms; K–8; adaptive; Reading + Math.
  - **It's both an assessment *and* an instructional platform** ("i-Ready Personalized Instruction" teaches K–8 math/reading) → a **direct competitor to Timeback**.
  - The **only one of the three administrable outside a single school** (authorized-provider model, e.g., Think Academy).
  - TEA-approved (Grade 7 reading + group-administered list); TEKS-aligned & TIA-approved; a **Texas–STAAR correlation study spanned 12 TX districts / 122,000+ students**.
  - **Adoption is outside Austin:** documented TX users include Grapevine-Colleyville (DFW), Dallas ISD, Tyler ISD (East TX), Sheldon ISD (Houston), United ISD (Laredo). **No major Austin-metro district was found using it.** National strongholds are California (LAUSD, Fresno, Oakland) and Florida; usage is elementary-weighted (grades 3–8).
- **DOK 2 — Summary:** Big national usage and a strong growth model, but the wrong fit for *our* credibility goal: it's effectively absent from the Austin metro, elementary-weighted, invisible to the public as a credential, and — because it also sells a competing K–8 curriculum — an awkward, non-neutral "judge" for Timeback. Its one real edge is out-of-school administration; keep it only as a fallback if that becomes decisive.
- **Links:** [Curriculum Associates — 14M students](https://www.curriculumassociates.com/about/press-releases/2025/09/super-stretch-schools-2025) · [EdSource — i-Ready (>13M)](https://edsource.org/2026/i-ready-concerns-student-anxiety/757967) · [i-Ready growth model](https://www.curriculumassociates.com/programs/i-ready-assessment/admin-resources/diagnostic) · [i-Ready–STAAR correlation brief, Texas (PDF)](https://cdn.bfldr.com/LS6J0F7/at/mkf6kw93p65k8rh5b5qm3pvm/iready-diagnostic-full-correlation-brief-texas.pdf) · [GCISD adopts i-Ready](https://www.gcisd.net/article/1713189) (accessed Aug 3, 2026)

### Test 5 — TerraNova / Iowa Assessments *(honorable mention)*
- **Sources:** Seton Testing & homeschool guides; Catholic-diocese assessment coverage; TEA approved-tests list.
- **DOK 1 — Facts:**
  - **TerraNova** (Data Recognition Corp, formerly CTB/McGraw-Hill; descendant of the California Achievement Test) is a nationally normed **achievement battery** (reading, language, math, science, social studies), K–12, 2017 norms.
  - Mostly used by **private/Catholic (diocesan) schools** for accountability (e.g., Archdioceses of Louisville and Omaha) and by **homeschoolers** for state compliance (parent-administerable without a bachelor's degree; accepted in most states).
  - TEA-approved on the group-administered list (K–12). **Iowa Assessments** (HMH/Riverside) are similar and used for gifted identification.
  - **Point-in-time achievement**, not an adaptive season-growth instrument.
- **DOK 2 — Summary:** A niche option with some recognition among the private-school/homeschool slice of the audience but unknown to the general public — and it measures achievement, not season growth. Honorable mention only; not a contender for the main measure.
- **Links:** [Seton Testing — TerraNova 3](https://www.setontesting.com/product/terranova-3/) · [TerraNova homeschool guide](https://numaschool.com/learn/glossary/terranova-test) · [Catholic schools use TerraNova (Kentucky Standard)](https://www.pmg-ky2.com/kystandard/archives/private-schools-use-act-terranova-test-for-accountability/article_0306b8fb-2b60-5dd9-91d3-07b160794093.html) · [TEA — approved group-administered tests (PDF)](https://tea.texas.gov/student-assessment/student-assessment-overview/state-approved-group-administered-achievement-tests.pdf) (accessed Aug 3, 2026)

## Bottom line for the call
If the #1 goal is a **verifiable proof we can actually market in the Austin metro without a licensing fight**, lead with **SAT / PSAT 8/9** (public proof + gate) backed by **Renaissance Star** (the locally-recognized, neutral growth engine). i-Ready is the fallback only if out-of-school access wins the day; MAP is the strongest local option the moment Legal can clear it. Whatever we pick, the **commercial-use licensing sign-off — not the test quality — is the critical path**, so start that conversation now.
